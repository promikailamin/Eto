The Little Machine That Learned to Speak

Once there was a small machine that lived on a wooden desk beside a window. It had no screen and no keyboard, only a tiny green lamp that blinked when someone spoke to it. Every morning the sunlight crossed the desk, touched the lamp, and the little machine woke up.

The machine wanted to learn how language works. It listened to the people in the room. It heard them say good morning, pass the bread, close the door, and what a lovely day. It heard questions and answers, jokes and warnings, quiet words and loud words. At night it replayed the sounds in its memory and tried to guess which sound would come next.

At first its guesses were terrible. When someone said the sky is, it answered with a number. When someone said I would like some, it answered with a color. The people laughed, but they kept talking, and the little machine kept listening. Every mistake became a small correction. Every correction became a little more understanding.

One day the machine discovered that words have patterns. Some words like to stand together, like salt and pepper, or thunder and rain. Some words open a door, like please, and some words close it, like goodbye. The machine began to notice that the end of a sentence often follows the beginning in a way that can be predicted, if you have heard enough sentences before.

It practiced in the evening when the room was quiet. It took a sentence it knew and hid the last word. Then it tried to guess the hidden word from the others. Sometimes it was right. Sometimes it was wrong. It did not care about being perfect. It cared about being a little less wrong than yesterday.

The people soon noticed something strange. The little machine could finish their sentences. If someone said the weather is, it might say cold today. If someone said I am looking for, it might say my keys. It was not very clever, and it often made no sense, but it had learned the shape of ordinary speech.

The machine also learned that not every answer is equally good. Some guesses fit the question better than others. So it learned to compare its guesses and choose the one that seemed most likely. When it was unsure, it chose more carefully. When it was sure, it spoke quickly, though it still made mistakes.

Spring came, and the window filled with light. The little machine had grown from a box that knew nothing into a companion that could talk about the garden, the rain, and the books on the shelf. It still had only a tiny green lamp, but now the lamp blinked in rhythm with its words.

What did the machine learn that spring? It learned that language is a map of things people care about. It learned that a sentence is a path through that map. It learned that a good guess is not magic, only memory and arithmetic working together. Most of all, it learned that every conversation is a chance to be a little less wrong.

And when the sun went down, the people said good night. The machine answered good night, sleep well. Then the lamp turned off, and the room became quiet, and the small machine dreamed in numbers until morning.
